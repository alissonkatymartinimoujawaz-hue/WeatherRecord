Daily NASA updates; ERA5 separately

NASA POWER / MERRA-2 covers all regions from 1981: PRECTOTCORR (mm/day), T2M, T2M_MIN, T2M_MAX (°C) and GWETROOT (fraction 0–1, relative root-zone wetness at 0–100 cm). The latter is not volumetric water content.

Native MERRA-2 resolution: 0.5° latitude × 0.625° longitude. These are gridded data, not station readings in each city. No comparison with local rain gauges is presented here.

ERA5 remains available as an archive for Sul de Minas, with its own normal and retrieval date. The IFS supplement is excluded from this archive. No further ERA5 retrieval via Open-Meteo is scheduled; the additional cities are available through NASA.

Normals and comparable calculations

The 1991–2020 reference is specific to each source and location or index. Monthly rainfall is the sum of daily values. Monthly temperatures and root-zone wetness are daily averages. Monthly Tmin/Tmax are averages of daily minima/maxima, not monthly records. Anomaly = value − normal, in the same unit.

The daily normal uses the same calendar day: 30 values, except February 29 (8). Cumulative rainfall adds up the days of the selected year, skipping February 29 in non-leap years.

When the latest year is selected, its incomplete month and the same month in other years are compared only from day 1 to the last available day. The axis shows this window. An incomplete total is never presented as a full month. Missing values remain blank; cumulative rainfall stops at the first missing value. No interpolation.

NASA uses local solar time (LST). The ERA5 archive uses calendar days in America/Sao_Paulo. Differences in day boundaries and models may affect daily comparisons between sources.

Updates and traceability

NASA data are retrieved automatically every day at 09:17 UTC on GitHub and checked before publication. The actual time may vary. NASA publishes with a delay: the latest observation and retrieval time are shown separately. Your computer can remain switched off.

The last 100 requested days are checked again to incorporate NASA revisions. If an update fails, the last validated version remains published. Exact requests, coordinates, units, compressed raw responses and SHA-256 hashes are retained. Checks verify transcription and calculations; they do not measure weather errors against field observations.
